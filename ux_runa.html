01 — Context

Overview

Runa and the Chaikuru Legacy is an action-adventure game rooted in Andean mythology, where players explore ancient ruins, restore forgotten monuments, and collect sacred artifacts tied to the Chaikuru lineage. The full UI/UX system was designed to feel like an extension of that world — every menu, icon, and transition reinforces the game's cultural identity instead of sitting on top of it as generic game-chrome.

The Problem

  • Generic game UI patterns clash with a culturally-rooted narrative
  • Multiple disconnected systems — menus, inventory, trading, progression
  • Need for clarity under gameplay pressure without losing atmosphere
  • Iconography had to read instantly while staying true to Andean motifs

The Goal

Build a cohesive UI/UX system across every player-facing screen — main menu, in-game HUD, merchant interface, artifact codex, and monument / key item tracking — so the interface feels carved from the same world as the game itself, while staying fast and legible in real-time play.

02 — Process

05 — In-Game Menu

During Play

The in-game pause menu had to interrupt action cleanly — readable at a glance, fast to dismiss, and visually consistent with the main menu without repeating its full ornamentation.

06 — Merchant & Artifacts

Trade & Collection

The merchant interface and the artifacts codex share the same "ancient book" framing device — every relic the player collects is logged into a living book UI, animated to feel like pages of real parchment turning.

📖
The Living Codex
Every artifact, monument, and key item the player discovers is recorded into an in-world book — turning inventory management into a piece of lore itself, rather than a flat list bolted onto the HUD.
07 — Monuments & Key Items

Tracking Progress

Monuments and key items act as the game's macro-progression layer — large landmarks the player restores across the world, each tied to its own icon, state, and unlock condition inside the UI.
